# Automation Brief: End Term, Problem 1 / Priya

## Why

**Pain:** Priya has been fundraising for three months and takes three to four investor meetings a week. Before each one she Googles the investor, scrolls their LinkedIn posts, checks the firm's portfolio page and reads interviews, then patches notes together. That is about 45 minutes of prep per 45-minute meeting, so nearly **three hours a week** go on research instead of product and team. The quality is uneven (it depends on how active the investor is online) and the cost of an error is high: repeating a wrong fact about someone's portfolio to that person.

**User:** Priya Raghunathan, founder of Sattva Kitchen, a D2C millet-food brand (a fictional founder, with a realistic profile, used for the demo).

**Outcome:** A structured, sourced brief within two minutes of entering the investor's name and firm: recent portfolio activity (last 90 days), publicly stated thesis, context worth knowing, and three talking points tailored to her stage and sector. Target: prep time from ~45 minutes to under 5 minutes of reading, with every claim traceable to a source.

## Journey Today

1. A meeting is booked with an investor she may not know well.
2. She Googles the name and firm and opens a dozen tabs.
3. She scrolls the investor's LinkedIn and X posts for stated views.
4. She checks the firm site for new portfolio announcements and reads recent interviews or blog posts.
5. She pastes fragments into a note, unsure which items are recent or accurate.
6. About 45 minutes later she has a patchy note and no tailored angle, and repeats this three to four times a week.

## Journey With the Tool

1. She opens PitchPrep, types the investor's name and firm (nothing else is required) and presses Generate.
2. She watches live progress: searching, reading sources, extracting claims, verifying, writing talking points.
3. In about a minute she gets the brief: a 20-second skim, three talking points for her startup, 90-day deals, stated thesis, watch-outs, and firm facts.
4. Every line links to its source with the exact quote and date. Anything that could not be verified is left out and listed, with the reason.
5. She reads it on her phone or saves a PDF, decides which angle to use and walks in prepared.
6. If a claim looks wrong she taps "Wrong?", which feeds the evaluation set.
7. Before the meeting she opens the phone-sized meeting card; if her calendar is connected the brief was already waiting.
8. After it she saves notes and a status in her pipeline, drafts a follow-up email from them (checked against her own numbers), edits it and sends it herself, and practises the hard questions before the next investor.
